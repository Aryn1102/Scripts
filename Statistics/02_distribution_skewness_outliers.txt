# Statistics — Lesson 2: Distribution, Skewness & Outliers

## Distribution

A distribution describes **how values are spread across a dataset**.

It helps us understand:

* Central tendency
* Spread
* Skewness
* Outliers
* Data quality

---

## Normal Distribution

A normal distribution is approximately **bell-shaped and symmetric**.

In an ideal normal distribution:

```text
Mean ≈ Median ≈ Mode
```

Data is distributed relatively evenly around the center.

Not every ML feature needs to follow a normal distribution.

---

## Skewness

Skewness measures the **asymmetry of a distribution**.

### Right / Positive Skew

The distribution has a **long tail toward larger values**.

```text
Mean > Median
```

Example:

```text
10, 11, 12, 12, 13, 14, 15, 50
```

### Left / Negative Skew

The distribution has a **long tail toward smaller values**.

```text
Mean < Median
```

Important:

> Positive/negative skewness refers to the **direction of the tail**, not whether the data values themselves are positive or negative.

---

## Outliers

An outlier is an observation that is **unusually far from the rest of the data**.

Example:

```text
20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 100
```

`100` may be an outlier.

An outlier is **not automatically an error**.

It may represent:

* Genuine extreme observation
* Measurement error
* Data-entry error
* Fraud
* Rare event

---

## Why Outliers Matter

Outliers can strongly affect:

* Mean
* Variance
* Standard deviation
* Linear regression
* Distance-based algorithms
* Some clustering methods

They can pull the mean away from the typical value.

---

# IQR Method

IQR = **Interquartile Range**

```text
IQR = Q3 - Q1
```

Where:

```text
Q1 = 25th percentile
Q3 = 75th percentile
```

IQR represents the spread of the **middle 50% of the data**.

### Outlier Boundaries

```text
Lower Bound = Q1 - 1.5 × IQR

Upper Bound = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR
```

Values outside these boundaries are commonly flagged as potential outliers.

### Example

```text
Q1 = 20
Q3 = 40
```

```text
IQR = 40 - 20
    = 20
```

```text
Lower Bound = 20 - 1.5(20)
            = -10

Upper Bound = 40 + 1.5(20)
            = 70
```

Potential outliers:

```text
x < -10
or
x > 70
```

---

# Z-Score

A z-score tells us **how many standard deviations a value is away from the mean**.

```text
z = (x - mean) / standard deviation
```

Example:

```text
Mean = 100
SD = 20
x = 140
```

```text
z = (140 - 100) / 20
  = 2
```

Therefore, `140` is **2 standard deviations above the mean**.

---

# IQR vs Z-Score

| IQR                        | Z-Score                                   |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Based on Q1 and Q3         | Based on mean and standard deviation      |
| Robust to outliers         | Sensitive to outliers                     |
| Does not require normality | Most useful for approximately normal data |
| Useful for skewed data     | Useful for identifying distance from mean |
