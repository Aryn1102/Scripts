# Statistics — Lesson 1: Fundamentals

## What is Statistics?

Statistics is the process of **collecting, organizing, analyzing, interpreting, and presenting data**.

In ML, statistics helps us understand:

* Data distribution
* Central tendency
* Spread
* Outliers
* Relationships between variables
* Data quality

---

## Population

A **population** is the complete set of observations we are interested in studying.

Example:

All students in a college.

---

## Sample

A **sample** is a subset of the population used to study or estimate characteristics of the population.

Example:

500 students selected from the college.

```text
Population
    ↓
All students
    ↓
Sample
    ↓
Selected students
```

Why use a sample?

* Population may be too large
* Collecting all data may be expensive
* Collecting all data may take too much time

---

# Measures of Central Tendency

Central tendency describes the **typical or central value** of a dataset.

The three major measures are:

```text
Mean
Median
Mode
```

---

## Mean

Mean is the arithmetic average.

```text
Mean = Sum of all values / Number of values
```

Example:

```text
10, 20, 30, 40, 50
```

```text
Mean = (10 + 20 + 30 + 40 + 50) / 5
     = 30
```

### Problem with mean

Mean is sensitive to extreme values.

Example:

```text
10, 20, 30, 40, 100
```

The `100` pulls the mean upward.

---

## Median

Median is the **middle value after sorting the data**.

Example:

```text
10, 20, 30, 40, 50
```

Median:

```text
30
```

For an even number of observations, take the average of the two middle values.

Example:

```text
10, 20, 30, 40
```

```text
Median = (20 + 30) / 2
       = 25
```

### Why median is useful

Median is more resistant to extreme values than the mean.

Example:

```text
10, 20, 30, 40, 1000
```

The median remains:

```text
30
```

while the mean is heavily affected by `1000`.

---

## Mode

Mode is the **most frequently occurring value**.

Example:

```text
10, 20, 20, 30, 40
```

Mode:

```text
20
```

A dataset can have:

* One mode
* Multiple modes
* No mode

---

# Mean vs Median vs Mode

| Measure | Meaning             | Sensitive to outliers? |
| ------- | ------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Mean    | Average             | Yes                    |
| Median  | Middle value        | Much less              |
| Mode    | Most frequent value | No direct effect       |

---

# Measures of Spread

Central tendency alone isn't enough.

Consider:

```text
A = 48, 49, 50, 51, 52
```

and:

```text
B = 10, 30, 50, 70, 90
```

Both have:

```text
Mean = 50
```

But their spread is very different.

Important measures of spread:

```text
Range
Variance
Standard deviation
```

---

# Range

Range is the difference between the maximum and minimum values.

```text
Range = Maximum - Minimum
```

Example:

```text
10, 20, 30, 40, 50
```

```text
Range = 50 - 10
      = 40
```

Range is simple but can be strongly affected by extreme values.

---

# Variance

Variance measures how far observations are spread around the mean.

Conceptually:

```text
Variance = average squared deviation from the mean
```

The important idea:

> Larger variance → data is more spread out.

> Smaller variance → data is more concentrated around the mean.

Variance uses **squared differences**, so its unit is squared.

---

# Standard Deviation

Standard deviation is the square root of variance.

```text
Standard deviation = √Variance
```

It tells us approximately how spread out the observations are around the mean.

The major advantage:

**Standard deviation has the same unit as the original data.**

Example:

If income is measured in ₹:

```text
Standard deviation → ₹
Variance            → ₹²
```

This makes standard deviation easier to interpret.
